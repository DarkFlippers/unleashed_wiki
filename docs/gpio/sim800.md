# SIM800 GSM module

::: tip Unleashed
**SIM800** is a built-in Unleashed app by @xMasterX (`Apps → GPIO → SIM800`). It drives a SIM800C or SIM800L GSM
module over the GPIO UART: voice calls, SMS (including multipart & UCS2/Cyrillic), incoming SMS alerts, a phonebook
shared with the Archive (`Phone` tab), SMS presets, USSD, DTMF, the SIM inbox, a raw AT terminal and module power
control. Stock firmware has nothing like it.
:::

You need: a SIM800C or SIM800L board, a **2G** SIM card with the **PIN disabled** (do it in a phone first, the app
can't enter a PIN), an antenna, and the right power supply (read [Powering](#powering) before you connect anything).

::: warning Legal
Only call and text numbers you're allowed to, on a SIM you own, following your network's and country's rules.
:::

## Which board do you have?

SIM800C and SIM800L are the same modem to this app (same AT commands, nothing to configure). What matters is the board
around it:

| Board | Supply |
|---|---|
| **With a regulator** (SIM800L V2.0, SIM800C HAT, most SIM800C breakouts) | Flipper pin 1 (5 V), direct |
| **Bare module** (SIM800L core board, bare SIM800C) | ~4 V: pin 1 through two diodes, or a LiPo cell |

A regulator is the larger 8-pin chip with an inductor and capacitors near the power input; such boards are labelled
with a wide input range (`5V`, `5-20V`). Without one, the supply pin goes almost straight to the modem's VBAT.

The modem's VBAT range is **3.4–4.4 V** (some SIM800C vendor sheets say 3.6–4.2 V). **Aim for 4.0 V** and both are
satisfied.

::: danger
- **5 V kills a bare module** (over the 4.4 V maximum).
- **3.3 V from pin 9 is too low** for a bare module (under the 3.4 V minimum), so it never starts. Pin 9 has the current
  (1.2 A), but not the voltage.
:::

## Wiring

### Regulated board (SIM800L V2.0 and most SIM800C breakouts)

Labelled `SIM800L V2.0`, about 40 × 28 mm, pins `5V`, `VDD`, `GND`, `SIM_TXD`, `SIM_RXD`, `RST`.

| Flipper pin | Board pin | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 11 or 18 (GND) | GND | shared with the supply ground |
| 13 (TX) | SIM_RXD | direct, the board shifts levels |
| 14 (RX) | SIM_TXD | direct |
| 1 (5V) | 5V | see [Powering](#powering) |

No divider needed. Boards with a logic-level jumper (e.g. Waveshare SIM800C HAT): leave it on **3.3 V**.

### Bare module (SIM800L core board, ~25 × 23 mm)

Pins `NET VCC RST RXD TXD GND` on one edge, `GND MIC+ MIC- SPK+ SPK- DTR RING` on the other. `NET` is the antenna pad.

| Flipper pin | Board pin | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 11 or 18 (GND) | GND | shared with the supply ground |
| 13 (TX) | RXD | direct, or through a resistor (below) |
| 14 (RX) | TXD | direct, never needs a resistor |
| — | VCC | **3.4–4.4 V only, 4.0 V ideal** |
| — | NET | antenna |

The bare module has no level shifter; its logic runs at 2.8 V (`VDD_EXT`). The Flipper's 3.3 V TX is slightly over the
module's RXD limit (VIH max ≈ 3.1 V):

| Wiring of Flipper TX → RXD | Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | 3.3 V | over spec, what most people run, works |
| 1 kΩ in series | 3.3 V | current-limited, good middle ground |
| 1 kΩ + 2.2 kΩ to GND | 2.27 V | in spec but only ~0.3 V above VIH min (~1.96 V) |

**Start direct.** A divider can drop below VIH min with tolerances, and on a board that already shifts levels the
2.2 kΩ to GND fights the board's driver.

If you switch the app to **LPUART** in Settings, use **pin 15 for TX and pin 16 for RX** instead of 13/14.

### Everything together, bare module

```text
FLIPPER                                   SIM800C / SIM800L (bare)

pin 1  5V --[1N5401]--[1N5401]--+---------  VCC   3.8-4.1 V
                                |
                            +  ===  4700uF low-ESR
                                |
pin 11 GND ---------------------+---------  GND

pin 13 TX --[ 1k ]--+---------------------  RXD
                    |
                 [ 2.2k ]
                    |
                   GND

pin 14 RX -------------------------------- TXD

pin 4  PA4 -[ 1k ]------------------------  BOOT / PWRKEY
```

## Powering

The famous "2 A" is a **burst**, not a load: 577 µs of transmit every 4.615 ms (12.5% duty). The average on a voice call
is about **250 mA** at full transmit power, down to ~110 mA with good signal. The burst comes out of a capacitor at the
module, not out of the supply.

**Size the capacitor, not the supply.** One burst moves 2 A × 577 µs ≈ 1.15 mC; with the supply holding the 250 mA
average, the cap covers ~1.0 mC. For SIMCom's 350 mV droop limit:

```text
C = 1.0 mC / 0.35 V ≈ 2900 µF
```

Use **2200–4700 µF low-ESR** electrolytic right at the module's VCC/GND pads. The often-quoted 1000 µF gives about 1 V
of droop: from 4.0 V that reaches the 3.0 V shutdown floor, so the module resets on a half-empty supply.

### From the Flipper's 5 V pin

- **The app turns pin 1 on by itself** when it starts. It retries up to five times, because a few thousand µF of
  capacitance can brown out the boost converter on inrush. On exit it turns 5 V off again, unless 5 V was already
  requested before the app started.
- It also **parks pin 7 low** on start (the external CC1101 amplifier control pin, so a CC1101 left in TX is switched to
  RX) and releases it on exit.
- Pin 1 is rated **1.2 A**; Unleashed sets the charger's boost limit to 1.4 A with fault shutdown. A V2.0 board on a call
  pulls ~240 mA from pin 1, well inside the limit. This works in everyday use, not just on the bench.
- **Plug the module in with the Flipper off.** Charging the bulk capacitor on inrush is what trips the boost, not the
  transmit burst.
- Expect roughly 1.2 W during a call: a couple of hours from a full Flipper battery.

### Dropping 5 V to ~4 V for a bare module

Two silicon rectifiers in series, then the bulk capacitor, then VBAT. It works because the capacitor supplies the burst,
so the diodes only carry the average current and their drop stays stable.

```text
                      1N5401  1N5401
Flipper pin 1 (5V) -----|>|-----|>|-----+------ module VCC   (~4.0 V)
                                        |
                                      __|__  + side  <- plus goes HERE, not to 5 V
                                      _____  electrolytic, 2200-4700uF
                                        |    - side
Flipper pin 11/18 (GND) ----------------+------ module GND
```

The stripe on the capacitor marks the **minus** lead (the shorter one): minus to GND, plus to VCC.

| Diodes | At 20 mA (idle) | At 250 mA (call) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2× 1N4001 (1 A) | ~3.8 V | ~3.44 V | too low on a call |
| 1× 1N5401 (3 A) | ~4.55 V | ~4.4 V | over 4.4 V at idle |
| **2× 1N5401 (3 A)** | **~4.1 V** | **~3.8 V** | **in spec at both ends** |

1N5401, 1N5404 and 1N5408 are all fine (same body, 3 A). **Don't use small-signal diodes** like 1N4148: two of them can
drop 1.8 V and leave VBAT near 3.2 V, and the module dies during its first full-power network search. If two diodes put
you under 3.6 V, use one and check idle stays under 4.4 V.

Choosing the capacitor:

- **Capacitance** sets the charge droop: 1.0 mC / C (4700 µF → 210 mV, 2200 µF → 450 mV).
- **ESR** adds an instant step of 1.75 A × ESR (0.1 Ω → 175 mV). Use **low-ESR**. Two 2200 µF low-ESR in parallel
  (≈230 mV total) or one 4700 µF low-ESR.
- Rate it **10 V or 16 V**, not 6.3 V, mount it at the pads, add a 100 nF ceramic beside it if you have one.

Four things that bite:

1. **Measure VBAT at both ends of the load.** `Status` shows the module's own reading (`AT+CBC`): idle under 4.4 V, under
   load above 3.4 V. Around 3.2 V the module powers off when it transmits.
2. **Some bare boards already have two diodes** on the supply pin. Adding two more browns it out.
3. **Never use a resistor divider** for power: its output collapses under the transmit burst.
4. **The cap goes after the diodes**, at the module. On the 5 V side it does nothing useful.

A single LiPo cell (3.7 V nominal, 4.2 V full) needs no dropper at all. With any separate supply (LiPo, 4 V bench
supply, buck converter) tie its ground to a Flipper GND pin.

## First start

1. Power the Flipper off, wire the module, power on.
2. Open `Apps → GPIO → SIM800`. The app:
   - turns on 5 V and parks pin 7 low;
   - shows **Connecting** with the baud rate and a progress bar while it sends `AT`, enables text-mode SMS and caller ID
     and reads the carrier and signal;
   - pulses PWRKEY and retries if nothing answers and a PWRKEY pin is set.
3. The main menu: `Basic`, `Saved`, `Advanced`, `Add number`, `SMS presets`, `Settings`, `How to use`.

If the module reports `UNDER-VOLTAGE`, the app beeps and `Status` shows the voltage with `LOW!` for the rest of the
session: your supply is sagging, the usual reason for dropped calls.

### Bring-up without a SIM (recommended)

Leave the SIM out the first time. The module answers AT commands without a card, so you can verify wiring, baud rate
and power first. `Status` shows *No SIM*, carrier `--`, *Not registered* and no bars — correct with no card.

- `Advanced → Device info` should fill in maker, model, firmware and IMEI (ICCID and IMSI stay `--`). If they read
  back, TX/RX and baud rate are right.
- The voltage in `Status` is VBAT at the module pads: on a bare module this is what your diode dropper delivers
  (3.8–4.1 V wanted). On a regulated board it reads after the regulator (~4 V) and tells you nothing about the input.

Insert the SIM with the module powered down.

### If nothing answers: PWRKEY

On SIM800C breakouts the pin marked **`BOOT`** is the modem's PWRKEY. Many boards start by themselves; first test with
`BOOT` unconnected and check `Device info`. If the module is dead, pull `BOOT` to GND for 1–1.5 s and release (a push
button works), or let the app do it:

1. Wire `BOOT` to a free Flipper pin **through a 1 kΩ series resistor** (nothing to GND on this line):

   ```text
   Flipper pin 4 (PA4) --[ 1k ]---- BOOT / PWRKEY
   Flipper pin 11 GND  ------------ GND
   ```

2. `Settings → PWRKEY pin`: choose the pin you used (`Off` by default).
3. `Advanced → Power & reset → Power on (PWRKEY)`: holds the pin low for 1.2 s, waits 3 s, checks for an answer.

Offered pins: **2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 13, 14, 15, 16**.

| Pin | Already used by |
|---|---|
| 2, 3, 4, 5 | external CC1101 (MOSI, MISO, CS, SCK) |
| 6 | external CC1101 GDO0 |
| 7 | external CC1101 amplifier (parked low by the app) |
| 13, 14 | this app's UART on USART |
| 15, 16 | this app's UART on LPUART |

With an external CC1101 attached, use the UART pair the app is **not** using (15/16 on USART, 13/14 on LPUART). Without a
CC1101, pins 2–6 are free. The app refuses pins its own UART uses (*Pin in use*), but can't see a CC1101.

The pin is driven **open-drain** (low = assert, high = high-impedance) because PWRKEY idles at VBAT (~4 V) through the
module's pull-up. **Don't tie PWRKEY to GND permanently**: the module then powers itself on and off whenever VBAT dips
under 3.3 V, turning brownouts into a power-cycle loop.

## Settings

`Settings` in the app main menu:

| Item | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `UART` | USART (pins 13/14) or LPUART (pins 15/16) |
| `Baud` | 1200…230400, must match the module. Default **2400** (what SIM800C boards ship fixed at; others use 115200 or autobaud). Unknown? Use `Advanced → Detect baud` |
| `PWRKEY pin` | Pin that pulses PWRKEY, `Off` by default |
| `Notify` | Green LED + beep when an action finishes, red when it fails; ring tune with buzz and backlight nudge every 2 s on an incoming call; chime and `New SMS` popup (`Close` / `Inbox`) when a message arrives |
| `Echo` | Keep the module's command echo on (shows what was sent in the AT terminal) |

Settings are stored in `apps_data/sim800/sim800.conf`. Changing UART or baud reopens the port once, when you leave the
screen, and reports whether the module answered.

## Nothing on the UART

Work down the list:

1. **Loopback test:** short pin 13 to pin 14 with nothing else attached, open `Advanced → AT terminal`, send anything.
   If it comes back, the Flipper and Settings are fine and the problem is the wiring or module. If not, `Settings → UART`
   doesn't match your pins.
2. **TX/RX swapped?** Flipper 13 TX → module RXD, Flipper 14 RX → module TXD.
3. **Remove the TX divider** and wire pin 13 directly.
4. **Run `Advanced → Detect baud`.** It sweeps 1200–230400 and saves the rate that answers. A module fixed with `AT+IPR`
   can be as low as 2400.
5. **Shared ground** between Flipper, module and supply.

Once it answers, `Advanced → Power & reset → Store settings` saves the working rate in the module.

## Using the app

### Typing

All text screens use a two-page keyboard: the bottom-left key switches letters/symbols. Symbol rows:
`+ * # @ ! ? & ( )`, `" ' , . : ; = -`, `/ \ $ % ~`. Hold <kbd>OK</kbd> on a letter for its capital, hold it on `_` for
a space, hold <kbd>BACK</kbd> to delete.

### Basic

- **`Call`** — choose the number (`Type a number` or `Saved numbers` once the phonebook has entries), the call screen
  opens.
- **`SMS`** — choose the number, then the text (`Type new text` or `Use a preset`), and it's sent. The result screen
  offers `Save No.` (add to phonebook) and `Preset` (save the text).
- **`Status`** — carrier, signal (dBm), registration, SIM state and module supply voltage, refreshed every second;
  <kbd>OK</kbd> refreshes now.

Call screen (carrier, signal bars, number, timer):

| Button | Action |
|---|---|
| <kbd>OK</kbd> | End the call; `Re-dial` after it ended |
| <kbd>←</kbd> | Save the number to the phonebook |
| <kbd>→</kbd> | `Tones` (DTMF pad) once connected |
| <kbd>BACK</kbd> | Leave the screen and hang up |

The **Tones** pad has 12 keys: arrows move, <kbd>OK</kbd> sends the digit immediately, <kbd>BACK</kbd> returns to the
call without ending it.

### Saved (phonebook)

A file browser over `/ext/phonebook`. An entry opens a quick-launch screen with live carrier/signal:
<kbd>←</kbd> `SMS`, <kbd>OK</kbd> `Call` (`Re-dial` after a call), <kbd>→</kbd> `Edit` with `Change number`, `Rename`
(file name and `Name:` change together) and `Delete`.

`Add number` in the main menu stores a number without calling it: type the number, then the name.

The same entries appear in **Archive → Phone**; opening one launches SIM800 straight on that entry. Add one to
Favorites for one-press dialling.

### SMS presets

Message texts stored in `apps_data/sim800/presets` (not shown in the phonebook or Archive). `New preset` creates one;
an existing preset offers `Send to number`, `View text`, `Edit text`, `Rename`, `Delete`. Presets also appear under
`Use a preset` whenever the app asks for a message.

### Advanced

| Item | What it does |
|---|---|
| `Device info` | Maker, model, firmware, IMEI, ICCID, IMSI, SIM state, supply |
| `Network info` | Carrier, signal, registration, network time, own number, SMS centre |
| `SMS inbox` | Messages on the SIM: automatically merges multipart SMS and decodes UCS2/Cyrillic; open to read, reply or delete (deleting a multipart SMS removes all its parts) |
| `History` | `Incoming calls`, `Incoming SMS`, `Clear history`. Stored in `apps_data/sim800/history.log` (trims its older half past 8 KB). SMS text is logged only when you open the message in the inbox |
| `USSD code` | Sends a code like `*100#` and shows the reply (including UCS2/Cyrillic) |
| `DTMF tones` | Plays a digit string into an active call |
| `Audio` | `Speaker`, `Ringer`, `Mic gain` and audio channel of the module's analog path (saved to `sim800.conf` and re-applied on startup). Voice never goes over the UART: you need a speaker and electret mic soldered to `SPK+/SPK-` and `MIC+/MIC-` |
| `AT terminal` | Recent lines and a prompt for manual AT commands; <kbd>←</kbd> `Log` opens the full scrollback |
| `Detect baud` | Sweeps 1200–230400, keeps the rate that answers |
| `Set module baud` | Changes the module's rate with `AT+IPR`, follows it and saves with `AT&W`; reverts if the module doesn't answer. Use it to get off slow 2400 |
| `Power & reset` | `Power on (PWRKEY)`, `Reset module`, `Flight mode on`, `Flight mode off`, `Power off`, `Factory defaults`, `Store settings` |

## File formats

Phonebook entry `/ext/phonebook/<name>.phone`:

```text
Filetype: Flipper SIM800 Phonebook Entry
Version: 1
Name: Alice
Number: +15551234567
```

SMS preset `apps_data/sim800/presets/<name>.smspr`:

```text
Filetype: Flipper SIM800 SMS Preset
Version: 1
Text: On my way
```

History log `apps_data/sim800/history.log` (plain text):

```text
11-19 14:32  CALL  +15551234567
11-19 14:35  SMS   +15559876543  24/11/19,14:35:02+00
  Message body, indented so it stays with its header line
```
